# Finding the perfect subject

Well, first of all, I don't think there is such a thing, because we're still dealing with an art form here, and whether something is beautiful or good or perfect lies mainly in the eye of the beholder. Of course you can measure a certain "quality" in photography – you look at camera settings, exposure, editing, composition… BUT from my own experience I have to say that these "perfect" pictures are rather boring to me personally, because there are plenty of them. Especially in wildlife photography there are countless perfectly centered portraits of the rarest and most breathtaking species. However, I feel that many of these photos lack character, and what makes a photograph special gets lost, because the focus is mainly on the subject. It's much more the composition that makes an image really work, and for me the robin is the best example. There are a lot of robins here in Münster, and especially in the city they are very trusting. That gives you the chance to photograph these little birds in the wildest scenarios and poses, and usually quite easily. To me, many of my robin photos have a lot more impact than, for example, the portrait of the little bittern, which is perfect in itself – exposure, resolution, sharpness and so on are all spot on, but somehow something is missing for me. I don't mean to say you should stop looking for rare species as well, because that is very appealing too, but for me it's often a more successful day to come home with an unexpectedly good picture of a common species than with a mediocre photo of something rarer. Especially at the beginning of a photography journey, focusing on these inconspicuous species can quickly lead to great photos.
